Let's watch
deep sea tapes.

NOAA, MBARI and the cabled observatories publish their dive footage in full. In this experiment, we are exploring automatic coverage strategies to learn more about what moved when in the footage.

Prototype

  • Every name is one detector's guess: a shortlist, not an identification.
  • Most frames were never read - a sample of dives, a look a second.
  • Counts are upper bounds; one animal is often counted twice.

    04 / whose work this is

    The footage

    Filmed, published and paid for by NOAA Ocean Exploration, by MBARI, and by the Ocean Observatories Initiative's Regional Cabled Array at the University of Washington. Every picture on this page says which of the three it came from; the terms each one asks for are in the imprint below.

    The detector

    A YOLOv5 checkpoint trained by FathomNet on MBARI imagery. It knows 499 classes and was never trained on most of this footage. Nothing here is a model this project built.

    The names

    Every name on this page is an automated guess - one object detector's answer for one frame, not an identification, and often enough wrong. The ranks above those names come from the World Register of Marine Species, and the article links from Wikipedia.

    This tool

    Pixel Patrol reads image and video collections and reports what is in them; this is its deep-sea extension. That is Pixel Patrol herself, gone diving.
